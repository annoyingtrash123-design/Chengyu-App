# Sample entries for review (20)

Every future entry follows this format.

## 1. 画蛇添足
**huà shé tiān zú** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · negative · neutral  
Themes: Animals & plants; Success & failure › Efficiency & waste; Success & failure › Unintended Results; Culture & stories › Classic stories & fables

- **Literal:** draw · snake · add · foot
- **Natural:** Drawing a snake and adding feet to it
- **Meaning:** To spoil something by adding what isn't needed; to overdo it.  
  比喻做了多余的事，非但无益，反而弄巧成拙。
- **English equivalents:** *gild the lily*, *over-egg the pudding*
- **Example:** 这篇文章已经写得很好了，结尾再加一段总结反而是画蛇添足。  
  The essay was already excellent; adding a summary paragraph at the end just spoiled it.
- **Example:** 这个设计简洁大方，再加装饰就画蛇添足了。  
  The design is clean and elegant — adding decoration would be overdoing it.
- **Origin — 《战国策·齐策二》:** In the state of Chu, a man gave his attendants a jug of wine. It was too little for everyone, so they agreed that whoever first drew a snake on the ground would drink it. One man finished first and, jug in hand, boasted that he had time to add feet. Before he had finished the feet, another man completed his snake, snatched the jug and said, "Snakes have no feet — how can you give it feet?" and drank the wine.
- **Usage:** Predicate or object, e.g. 未免画蛇添足 / 是画蛇添足. Critical. Common when giving feedback on writing, design or speeches.
- **Synonyms:** 多此一举、弄巧成拙
- **Antonyms:** 恰到好处、画龙点睛

## 2. 守株待兔
**shǒu zhū dài tù** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · negative · neutral  
Themes: Animals & plants; Ambition & effort › Laziness and Giving Up; Behaviour & conduct › Resistance to Change; Culture & stories › Classic stories & fables

- **Literal:** guard · tree stump · wait for · rabbit
- **Natural:** Keeping watch by a tree stump, waiting for a rabbit
- **Meaning:** To wait idly for good luck instead of working for it; to cling rigidly to past experience.  
  比喻死守狭隘经验，不知变通；也比喻妄想不劳而获。
- **English equivalents:** *wait for something to fall into your lap*, *wait for pennies from heaven*
- **Example:** 找工作要主动出击，光在家守株待兔是不行的。  
  When job hunting you have to take the initiative — just sitting at home waiting for luck won't work.
- **Origin — 《韩非子·五蠹》:** A farmer in the state of Song saw a rabbit dash into a tree stump in his field, break its neck and die. From then on he put down his plough and waited beside the stump, hoping to catch another rabbit. No rabbit ever came, his fields went to ruin, and he became the laughing stock of Song.
- **Usage:** Predicate or adverbial. Critical. Often contrasted with 主动出击 (taking the initiative).
- **Synonyms:** 刻舟求剑、墨守成规
- **Antonyms:** 随机应变

## 3. 半途而废
**bàn tú ér fèi** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · negative · neutral  
Themes: Ambition & effort; Ambition & effort › Laziness and Giving Up; Success & failure › Near Misses and Setbacks

- **Literal:** half · road · and · abandon
- **Natural:** Giving up halfway along the road
- **Meaning:** To give up partway through; to leave something unfinished.  
  路走到一半就停下来。比喻做事有始无终，不能坚持到底。
- **English equivalents:** *give up halfway*, *fall at the last hurdle*
- **Example:** 学语言最怕半途而废，每天坚持一点点就会有进步。  
  The worst thing when learning a language is giving up halfway — a little every day adds up.
- **Example:** 既然开始了，就不要半途而废。  
  Now that you've started, don't give up halfway.
- **Origin — 《礼记·中庸》；故事见《后汉书·列女传》:** The Doctrine of the Mean says: "The gentleman follows the Way; to give up halfway — that I cannot do." A well-known story illustrates it: Yue Yangzi left home to study but came back after a year because he missed home. His wife took a knife to her loom and cut through the cloth she was weaving, saying: "This cloth is woven thread by thread. Cut it now and all the work is wasted. Abandoning your studies halfway is no different." Moved, he went back and finished his studies.
- **Usage:** Predicate. Critical. Very common, especially in advice: 不要半途而废 / 千万别半途而废.
- **Synonyms:** 有始无终、前功尽弃
- **Antonyms:** 持之以恒、有始有终、锲而不舍

## 4. 马到成功
**mǎ dào chéng gōng** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · positive · neutral  
Themes: Animals & plants; Success & failure › Successful Completion; War & strategy › Military Strategy and Victory; Culture & stories › Festivals and Celebrations

- **Literal:** horse · arrive · achieve · success
- **Natural:** Victory as soon as the horses arrive
- **Meaning:** To succeed immediately; as a wish, "may you succeed straight away".  
  战马一到就取得胜利。形容事情顺利，一开始就取得成功。多用作祝福语。
- **English equivalents:** *hit the ground running*, *break a leg (as a wish)*
- **Example:** 祝你这次比赛马到成功！  
  Good luck in the competition — may you win straight away!
- **Example:** 新产品一上市就马到成功，销量远超预期。  
  The new product was an instant success, selling far more than expected.
- **Origin — 元·无名氏《小尉迟》第二折:** In ancient warfare cavalry often decided the battle, so the phrase praised a general so capable that victory came the moment his warhorses reached the field. It already appears in Yuan-dynasty zaju plays and later became one of the most popular good-luck wishes.
- **Usage:** Mostly a blessing: 祝你……马到成功. Also a predicate. Popular before exams, interviews, matches and business openings, and especially in the Year of the Horse.
- **Synonyms:** 旗开得胜、一帆风顺
- **Antonyms:** 一败涂地

## 5. 乱七八糟
**luàn qī bā zāo** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · negative · casual  
Themes: Numbers & degree › Numbers; Behaviour & conduct › Order & chaos

- **Literal:** messy · seven · eight · bad
- **Natural:** All sevens and eights, in a mess
- **Meaning:** In a complete mess; chaotic and disorganised.  
  形容杂乱无章，乱得不成样子。
- **English equivalents:** *at sixes and sevens*, *higgledy-piggledy*, *a complete shambles*
- **Example:** 他的房间乱七八糟的，书和衣服扔得到处都是。  
  His room is a complete mess, with books and clothes thrown everywhere.
- **Example:** 别整天想那些乱七八糟的事，早点睡吧。  
  Stop worrying about all that random stuff and get some sleep.
- **Origin — 民间说法（无确切出处）:** A popular explanation links 七 and 八 to two periods of turmoil: the Rebellion of the Seven States (七国之乱) in the Western Han and the War of the Eight Princes (八王之乱) in the Western Jin. This is folk etymology; in practice 七…八… is simply a common pattern meaning "all kinds of, jumbled", as in 七上八下 and 七嘴八舌.
- **Usage:** Very colloquial. Works as a predicate, a complement (搞得乱七八糟, 写得乱七八糟) or an attributive (乱七八糟的东西, "all sorts of junk"). Describes thoughts and situations as well as physical mess.
- **Synonyms:** 杂乱无章、一塌糊涂
- **Antonyms:** 井井有条、有条不紊

## 6. 莫名其妙
**mò míng qí miào** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · neutral · casual  
Themes: Emotions & mind › Confusion and Doubt; Emotions & mind

- **Literal:** no one · can name · its · mystery
- **Natural:** No one can explain the secret of it
- **Meaning:** Baffling; inexplicable; for no apparent reason.  
  没有人能说出其中的奥妙。指事情很奇怪，说不出道理，使人不明白。
- **English equivalents:** *out of the blue*, *for no rhyme or reason*
- **Example:** 他莫名其妙地发了一顿脾气，大家都不知道为什么。  
  He lost his temper for no apparent reason, and nobody knew why.
- **Example:** 这部电影的结局真让人莫名其妙。  
  The ending of this film was totally baffling.
- **Usage:** Very common in speech. Predicate (真莫名其妙), adverbial (莫名其妙地……) or attributive. Often carries mild annoyance. The older variant 莫明其妙 is also seen.
- **Synonyms:** 不可思议、匪夷所思
- **Antonyms:** 恍然大悟、一目了然

## 7. 一举两得
**yī jǔ liǎng dé** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · positive · neutral  
Themes: Numbers & degree › Numbers; Success & failure › Efficiency & waste; Wisdom & planning

- **Literal:** one · move · two · gains
- **Natural:** One action, two gains
- **Meaning:** To gain two benefits from one action.  
  做一件事，同时得到两方面的好处。
- **English equivalents:** *kill two birds with one stone*
- **Example:** 骑自行车上班既能锻炼身体又能省钱，真是一举两得。  
  Cycling to work keeps you fit and saves money — it kills two birds with one stone.
- **Origin — 《晋书·束皙传》；故事见《史记·张仪列传》:** Bian Zhuangzi wanted to kill two tigers that were fighting over an ox. Someone advised him to wait: when two tigers fight, the smaller will die and the larger will be wounded. If he then killed the wounded one, he would earn the fame of killing two tigers in a single stroke. He followed the advice and won the name of a double tiger-slayer.
- **Usage:** Predicate or attributive (一举两得的办法). Positive; often describes a clever plan. In speech 一 changes to yí here (tone sandhi), but it is written yī.
- **Synonyms:** 一箭双雕、一石二鸟
- **Antonyms:** 得不偿失

## 8. 自相矛盾
**zì xiāng máo dùn** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · negative · neutral  
Themes: Speech & truth; Speech & truth › Deception and False Appearances; Culture & stories › Classic stories & fables

- **Literal:** self · mutually · spear · shield
- **Natural:** One's own spear and shield contradicting each other
- **Meaning:** To contradict oneself; self-contradictory.  
  比喻自己的言行前后抵触，不能自圆其说。
- **English equivalents:** *contradict yourself*, *blow hot and cold*
- **Example:** 他一会儿说没去过那里，一会儿又说在那里见过朋友，说法自相矛盾。  
  First he said he'd never been there, then that he'd met a friend there — his story contradicts itself.
- **Origin — 《韩非子·难一》:** A man of Chu was selling spears and shields. He boasted that his shields were so strong that nothing could pierce them, then that his spears were so sharp they could pierce anything. Someone asked, "What happens if I strike your shield with your spear?" The man had no answer.
- **Usage:** Predicate or attributive. Critical. Common in arguments, debates and essays. The everyday word 矛盾 "contradiction, conflict" comes from this story.
- **Synonyms:** 前后矛盾
- **Antonyms:** 自圆其说

## 9. 亡羊补牢
**wáng yáng bǔ láo** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · neutral · neutral  
Themes: Animals & plants; Wisdom & planning; Culture & stories › Classic stories & fables; Time › Poor Timing

- **Literal:** lose · sheep · mend · pen
- **Natural:** Mending the pen after losing a sheep
- **Meaning:** To fix a problem after a loss so it doesn't happen again; it's not too late to put things right.  
  羊丢了再去修补羊圈。比喻出了问题以后想办法补救，可以防止继续受损失。
- **English equivalents:** *better late than never*, *shut the stable door after the horse has bolted (but here it's worth doing)*
- **Example:** 这次没考好没关系，亡羊补牢，为时未晚，现在开始努力还来得及。  
  Don't worry about this test — it's not too late to put things right if you start working hard now.
- **Origin — 《战国策·楚策四》:** The minister Zhuang Xin warned King Xiang of Chu against luxury and flatterers, but the king ignored him. Later Qin attacked, and the king had to flee. Full of regret, he called Zhuang Xin back for advice. Zhuang Xin replied: "It is not too late to call the hound after seeing the hare, nor too late to mend the pen after losing a sheep."
- **Usage:** Often in the fixed phrase 亡羊补牢，为时未晚 "it's not too late to fix things". Encouraging, though it implies the loss has already happened. Contrast 未雨绸缪 (preparing before trouble comes).
- **Synonyms:** 知错就改
- **Antonyms:** 未雨绸缪、防患未然

## 10. 对牛弹琴
**duì niú tán qín** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · negative · neutral  
Themes: Animals & plants; Speech & truth; Animals & plants › Animal Behavior Metaphors; Culture & stories › Classic stories & fables

- **Literal:** to · ox · play · zither
- **Natural:** Playing the zither to an ox
- **Meaning:** To talk to someone who can't understand or appreciate it; to misjudge one's audience.  
  比喻对不懂道理的人讲道理，对外行人说内行话；也讥讽说话不看对象。
- **English equivalents:** *cast pearls before swine*, *like talking to a brick wall*
- **Example:** 跟他讲古典音乐简直是对牛弹琴，他只喜欢流行歌。  
  Talking to him about classical music is pointless — he only likes pop songs.
- **Origin — 汉·牟融《理惑论》:** The musician Gongming Yi played an elegant piece for an ox, which went on grazing, unmoved. It was not that the ox couldn't hear — the music simply didn't suit its ears. He then played sounds like buzzing gadflies and a lowing calf, and the ox at once flicked its tail, pricked up its ears and paced about, listening.
- **Usage:** Predicate or object. Can criticise the listener (too ignorant to understand) or the speaker (for not considering the audience). It compares someone to a cow, so it's rude to say to their face.
- **Synonyms:** 白费口舌
- **Antonyms:** 有的放矢

## 11. 胸有成竹
**xiōng yǒu chéng zhú** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★ Common · positive · neutral  
Themes: Animals & plants › Plant and Natural World; Wisdom & planning; Arts & craft › Excellent Writing

- **Literal:** chest · have · complete · bamboo
- **Natural:** Having the whole bamboo already in one's mind
- **Meaning:** To have a well-thought-out plan; to be confident of success.  
  画竹子之前，心里已经有了竹子的完整形象。比喻做事之前已有通盘考虑，很有把握。
- **English equivalents:** *have it all mapped out*, *have it in the bag*
- **Example:** 考试前她复习得很充分，所以走进考场时胸有成竹。  
  She had revised thoroughly, so she walked into the exam hall full of confidence.
- **Origin — 宋·苏轼《文与可画筼筜谷偃竹记》:** Wen Tong (style name Yuke), a Northern Song painter famous for bamboo, planted bamboo around his house and studied it closely through every season. His friend Su Shi wrote: "To paint bamboo, you must first have the complete bamboo in your mind."
- **Usage:** Predicate or adverbial (胸有成竹地说). Positive; calm confidence that comes from preparation. Also 成竹在胸.
- **Synonyms:** 成竹在胸、稳操胜券
- **Antonyms:** 束手无策

## 12. 塞翁失马
**sài wēng shī mǎ** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★ Common · neutral · neutral  
Themes: Animals & plants; Fortune & circumstances; Fortune & circumstances › Bad Fortune; Culture & stories › Classic stories & fables; Wisdom & planning

- **Literal:** frontier · old man · lose · horse
- **Natural:** The old man at the frontier loses his horse
- **Meaning:** A misfortune may turn out to be a blessing, and vice versa.  
  比喻一时虽然受到损失，也许反而因此得到好处；也指坏事在一定条件下可以变成好事，反之亦然。
- **English equivalents:** *a blessing in disguise*, *every cloud has a silver lining*
- **Example:** 虽然没被那所大学录取，但他在现在的学校遇到了最好的导师，真是塞翁失马，焉知非福。  
  He didn't get into that university, but at his current one he met the best supervisor — a real blessing in disguise.
- **Origin — 《淮南子·人间训》:** An old man living by the frontier lost his horse, which ran off into nomad territory. When the neighbours consoled him, he said, "How do you know this isn't a blessing?" Months later the horse came back with a fine nomad horse. When they congratulated him, he said, "How do you know this isn't a misfortune?" His son, riding the new horse, fell and broke his leg. When they consoled him again, he again asked how they knew it wasn't a blessing. A year later the nomads invaded; the young men were sent to fight and most died, but the son was spared because of his lame leg.
- **Usage:** Usually in the full saying 塞翁失马，焉知非福 "how do you know it isn't a blessing?". Used to comfort someone after a setback. 塞 is read sài here, not sāi or sè.
- **Synonyms:** 因祸得福

## 13. 自由自在
**zì yóu zì zài** · 成语 Chengyu · ★★★ Core · positive · casual  
Themes: Emotions & mind › Freedom & ease; Daily life › Relaxation and Rest

- **Literal:** self · follow · self · at ease
- **Natural:** Free and at ease, following one's own will
- **Meaning:** Free and unrestrained; carefree; doing as one pleases.  
  形容没有约束，十分安闲随意。
- **English equivalents:** *free as a bird*, *footloose and fancy-free*
- **Example:** 退休以后，爷爷每天种花钓鱼，过得自由自在。  
  Since retiring, Grandpa grows flowers and goes fishing every day, living a carefree life.
- **Example:** 小鸟在天空中自由自在地飞翔。  
  Birds fly freely across the sky.
- **Usage:** Very common in speech and writing. Predicate, complement (过得自由自在) or adverbial (自由自在地……). Pattern ABAC — note the repeated 自.
- **Synonyms:** 无拘无束、逍遥自在
- **Antonyms:** 身不由己

## 14. 三个臭皮匠，顶个诸葛亮
**sān gè chòu pí jiàng, dǐng gè zhū gě liàng** · 俗语 Proverb · ★★★ Core · positive · casual  
Themes: Relationships; Wisdom & planning; Numbers & degree › Numbers

- **Literal:** three · (measure word) · smelly · cobbler, equal · (measure word) · Zhuge Liang
- **Natural:** Three smelly cobblers are a match for one Zhuge Liang
- **Meaning:** Ordinary people pooling their ideas can match a genius.  
  比喻人多智慧多，几个普通人的智慧合起来，能顶一个聪明人。
- **English equivalents:** *two heads are better than one*, *many hands make light work*
- **Example:** 这个问题我们一起讨论吧，三个臭皮匠，顶个诸葛亮。  
  Let's work through this problem together — two heads are better than one.
- **Origin — 民间俗语:** Zhuge Liang (181–234), chancellor of Shu Han in the Three Kingdoms period, is the archetype of wisdom in Chinese culture. A popular theory holds that 皮匠 "cobbler" was originally 裨将 pí jiàng "deputy general" — three deputy generals together could match Zhuge Liang — and the words merged in speech because they sound alike.
- **Usage:** Said to encourage teamwork and brainstorming. Humorous and modest (you call your own group "smelly cobblers"). Also 三个臭皮匠，赛过诸葛亮.
- **Synonyms:** 集思广益、众人拾柴火焰高

## 15. 吃一堑，长一智
**chī yī qiàn, zhǎng yī zhì** · 俗语 Proverb · ★★★ Core · positive · neutral  
Themes: Learning & teaching; Wisdom & planning › Experience and Old Age; Success & failure › Near Misses and Setbacks

- **Literal:** eat · one · pit, grow · one · wisdom
- **Natural:** Fall into a pit once and you grow a little wiser
- **Meaning:** Every setback makes you wiser; you learn from your mistakes.  
  受一次挫折，便长一分见识。
- **English equivalents:** *once bitten, twice shy*, *live and learn*
- **Example:** 上次因为没备份丢了论文，吃一堑，长一智，现在我每天都备份文件。  
  Last time I lost my essay because I hadn't backed it up. Lesson learned — now I back up my files every day.
- **Origin — 明·王守仁《与薛尚谦书》:** The idea goes back to a letter by the Ming philosopher Wang Shouren (Wang Yangming): "Whoever stumbles once gains a bit of wisdom; today's loss may well become tomorrow's gain." The rhyming spoken form 吃一堑，长一智 later became the usual version.
- **Usage:** Used after a mistake, to sum up the lesson or to comfort someone. 堑 qiàn ("moat, pit") is rare outside this saying; 长 is read zhǎng ("grow"), not cháng.
- **Synonyms:** 失败是成功之母
- **Antonyms:** 重蹈覆辙

## 16. 百闻不如一见
**bǎi wén bù rú yī jiàn** · 俗语 Proverb · ★★★ Core · neutral · neutral  
Themes: Numbers & degree › Numbers; Learning & teaching; Daily life › Journeys and Exploration

- **Literal:** hundred · hear · not · as good as · one · see
- **Natural:** Hearing something a hundred times is not as good as seeing it once
- **Meaning:** Seeing something for yourself beats hearing about it many times.  
  听别人说一百次，不如亲眼看一次。指亲眼看到的比听来的更可靠。
- **English equivalents:** *seeing is believing*
- **Example:** 大家都说桂林山水美，这次亲自去了才知道，真是百闻不如一见。  
  Everyone says Guilin's scenery is beautiful, but only after going myself did I really understand — seeing is believing.
- **Origin — 《汉书·赵充国传》:** When the Qiang people rose up in the northwest, Emperor Xuan of Han asked the general Zhao Chongguo, then over seventy, how many troops would be needed. Zhao replied: "Hearing a hundred times is not as good as seeing once. It is hard to plan a campaign from afar — let me ride to Jincheng and draw up a plan there." He studied the terrain in person and proposed stationing soldiers to farm the frontier, which eventually brought peace to the region.
- **Usage:** Said when something seen in person turns out better than (or different from) what you'd heard. Common in travel talk. Sometimes extended: 百闻不如一见，百见不如一干 ("seeing beats hearing; doing beats seeing").
- **Synonyms:** 眼见为实
- **Antonyms:** 道听途说

## 17. 外甥打灯笼——照舅（旧）
**wài sheng dǎ dēng long —— zhào jiù (jiù)** · 歇后语 Two-part saying · ★★ Common · neutral · casual  
Themes: Fortune & circumstances › Change; Behaviour & conduct › Resistance to Change; Speech & truth

- **Literal:** nephew · carry · lantern — light up · uncle (pun: as before)
- **Natural:** A nephew carrying a lantern lights the way for his uncle
- **Meaning:** Same as before; nothing has changed. A pun: 照舅 "light up (his) uncle" sounds like 照旧 "as before".  
  谐音"照旧"，意思是和原来一样，没有变化。
- **English equivalents:** *same old, same old*, *plus ça change*
- **Example:** 他说要改掉晚睡的习惯，结果一个月过去了，还是外甥打灯笼——照旧。  
  He said he'd break his late-night habit, but a month later it's "a nephew with a lantern" — same as ever.
- **Usage:** A homophone 歇后语: the punchline 照舅 zhào jiù ("shine for uncle") sounds identical to 照旧 zhào jiù ("as before"). People often say only the first half and let the listener fill in the rest. Humorous and colloquial.
- **Synonyms:** 一如既往、依然如故
- **Antonyms:** 焕然一新

## 18. 竹篮打水——一场空
**zhú lán dǎ shuǐ —— yī chǎng kōng** · 歇后语 Two-part saying · ★★★ Core · negative · casual  
Themes: Success & failure › Efficiency & waste; Success & failure › Complete Failure and Obscurity; Daily life › Broken or Useless Items

- **Literal:** bamboo · basket · fetch · water — one · round · empty
- **Natural:** Fetching water with a bamboo basket — it all comes to nothing
- **Meaning:** All that effort wasted; ending up with nothing.  
  比喻白费力气，毫无收获。
- **English equivalents:** *all for nothing*, *a wild-goose chase*, *pouring water into a sieve*
- **Example:** 他炒股赔光了所有积蓄，到头来竹篮打水——一场空。  
  He lost all his savings on the stock market — in the end it was fetching water with a bamboo basket: nothing left.
- **Usage:** A logic-based 歇后语 (no pun): water runs straight through a bamboo basket. Very common; often only the first half is said. 场 is read chǎng here.
- **Synonyms:** 白费力气、前功尽弃
- **Antonyms:** 满载而归

## 19. 拍马屁
**pāi mǎ pì** · 惯用语 Idiom phrase · ★★★ Core · negative · casual  
Themes: Animals & plants; Character & looks › Negative Character Flaws; Society & power › Leadership and Authority

- **Literal:** pat · horse · backside
- **Natural:** Patting the horse's backside
- **Meaning:** To flatter someone shamelessly to win favour.  
  比喻谄媚奉承，讨好别人。
- **English equivalents:** *suck up to*, *butter someone up*, *brown-nose*
- **Example:** 他整天拍老板的马屁，同事们都看不惯。  
  He's always sucking up to the boss, and his colleagues can't stand it.
- **Example:** 别拍马屁了，有什么事直说吧。  
  Stop buttering me up — just tell me what you want.
- **Origin — 相传源于元代蒙古族习俗:** Tradition says that in the Yuan dynasty, when Mongols leading horses met, they would pat the other's horse on the rump and praise it as a fine horse, as a courtesy to its owner. Some people praised every horse regardless of quality to curry favour, and "patting the horse's backside" came to mean flattery.
- **Usage:** A separable verb-object phrase: 拍某人的马屁, 拍了半天马屁. Very colloquial and derogatory. A 马屁精 is a suck-up; 拍马屁拍到马腿上 is flattery that backfires.
- **Synonyms:** 阿谀奉承、溜须拍马
- **Antonyms:** 直言不讳

## 20. 走后门
**zǒu hòu mén** · 惯用语 Idiom phrase · ★★★ Core · negative · casual  
Themes: Society & power; Society & power › Corruption and Injustice

- **Literal:** walk · back · door
- **Natural:** Going in through the back door
- **Meaning:** To get something through connections or influence rather than proper channels.  
  比喻通过托人情、拉关系等不正当途径达到目的。
- **English equivalents:** *pull strings*, *it's not what you know, it's who you know*
- **Example:** 他靠走后门进了这家公司，大家都不服气。  
  He got into the company through connections, and nobody thinks it's fair.
- **Example:** 现在招生很透明，想走后门可没那么容易。  
  Admissions are very transparent now; it's not easy to pull strings.
- **Usage:** Separable: 走了后门, 走他的后门. Used of jobs, school places, permits and so on obtained through 关系 (connections). Critical. The person granting the favour 开后门 ("opens the back door").
- **Synonyms:** 拉关系、托关系
- **Antonyms:** 公事公办
